Why an air conditioner leaks

AC leak water damage in Wheeling, IL almost always starts with condensation. As the system cools air, it pulls moisture out and sends it down a condensate line to a drain. When that line clogs with algae or debris, the drain pan overflows and water goes to the nearest floor or ceiling. A closet air handler can soak the closet floor and the wall behind it.

A common call is a wet spot on a closet ceiling or floor near the unit. A summer humidity spike makes it worse, and a unit running all day produces more condensate. If water is dripping, switch the system off at the thermostat and place a bucket under the leak before you call.

What we do after an AC leak

The crew locates the water with a moisture meter and thermal camera, then dries the structure. A clogged condensate line is an HVAC repair, so an HVAC technician clears or replaces it while we handle the wet drywall and flooring. If the leak ran for weeks, the same moisture can feed mold growth, and the mold remediation page explains testing and removal.

Water that dripped into a ceiling is covered in more depth on the ceiling water damage page. Water that reached the closet floor often wicks into carpet, so the carpet page is a useful next read. Early action matters, since condensate that sits in insulation stays damp for days.

Mold risk after a slow AC leak

A condensate leak is warm, humid and slow, which is the setup mold needs. Drywall, insulation and carpet that stay damp for days can start growing it, and a closet or attic hides the evidence. If the stain smells musty or you see dark spots, mention it on the call, because it changes the plan from a simple dry-out to containment and removal.

We do not diagnose health effects, and questions about symptoms belong to a physician. What we can do is measure moisture, dry the structure and tell you plainly whether visible growth needs the process described on the mold page.

What to tell us when you call about an AC leak

  • Where the unit sits, such as attic, closet, basement or roof
  • Where you see the water and when you first noticed it
  • Whether the system has been running all day or only part of it
  • Any dark spots, musty smell or sagging drywall near the stain
  • Whether an HVAC technician has already looked at the line

Condensate lines and drain pans are routine HVAC maintenance, and a technician can also install a float switch that shuts the system off when the pan fills. That is a cheap safeguard against a repeat. It is not part of restoration work, but it is worth asking about when the line is cleared.

What affects AC leak cleanup cost

AC leaks tend to be slow, so cost depends on how long the water ran:

  • Weeks of slow leaking versus a single overflow
  • Ceiling or flooring area wet
  • Insulation that must be removed
  • Whether mold growth has started
  • Drying days and equipment

AC leak questions

Is AC water dangerous?

Condensate is not sewage, but it can carry algae and dust, and standing water can feed mold. Dry it promptly.

Can I clear the condensate line myself?

Some homeowners can, but a technician is the safer choice. The drying still needs a restoration crew.

Does this happen only in summer?

It is most common in cooling season, but a clogged line can cause damage any time the system runs.
