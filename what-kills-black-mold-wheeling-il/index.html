What Kills Black Mold? A Wheeling, IL Homeowner Guide

"Black mold" is a nickname, not a single organism, and the question behind it is usually practical: what actually gets rid of it? Here is what works, what does not and where the line is.

What "black mold" means

People use the phrase for any dark mold, usually on walls, grout or ceilings. Color alone cannot tell you the type, and a lab test is the only way to identify a species. For a homeowner deciding what to do, the type matters less than three practical questions: how much is there, what is it growing on and where does the moisture come from?

We do not discuss health effects here. Questions about symptoms belong to a physician, and the EPA tells readers with health concerns to consult a health professional before starting cleanup.

What works on hard surfaces

The EPA's guidance for hard surfaces is simple. Scrub the mold off with detergent and water, then dry the surface completely. That is the baseline for tile, glass, sealed countertops and similar materials. It removes the growth physically, which matters more than trying to chemically destroy it.

  • Soap or detergent and water, with scrubbing
  • Complete drying, with air movement or a dehumidifier
  • Fixing the moisture that fed it
  • Discarding the cloths used, in a bag

Chemical products can add risks, and mixing cleaners can produce dangerous fumes. Never combine cleaning products, and follow every label.

What does not work

Painting over mold does not kill it. The EPA says not to paint or caulk moldy surfaces, since paint over mold is likely to peel. Spraying a disinfectant on porous drywall or wood and walking away leaves the growth in place and the moisture untouched. Sealing a damp wall with primer can trap the problem inside.

Why porous materials are different

Drywall, ceiling tile, insulation and carpet have tiny spaces where growth can settle in. The EPA notes that items like ceiling tiles and carpet may need to be discarded because mold can grow into those spaces and may be impossible to remove fully. That is why professionals cut out affected drywall instead of trying to disinfect it.

Removal is done with containment, so spores are not spread to clean rooms. Plastic barriers seal the work area, negative air machines with HEPA filters control the air, and debris is bagged before it leaves the space. The steps are described on the mold remediation page.

How much is too much

The EPA describes an area under about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot square, as something a homeowner can often handle. Larger growth, sewage or other contaminated water, or a possible problem in the heating and cooling system are reasons to hire a professional. Look at a wall where one stain suggests a bigger wet area behind it, and have it checked.

Stopping the moisture

The only lasting answer to what kills mold is a dry building. Repair leaks, keep gutters clear, vent bathroom and dryer air outside and keep indoor humidity in check. If a basement stays damp, a dehumidifier helps, but a persistent damp wall can mean groundwater, and that needs a drainage fix beyond restoration.

A dry wall reading, taken with a meter and compared with a dry reference, is the most useful thing you can have. The structural drying page explains how crews confirm it.

A Wheeling note

Illinois passed Public Act 103-0893, effective January 1, 2025, amending the Mold Remediation Registration Act. As we read it, the Department of Public Health may build a registration program asking for third-party certification and proof of financial responsibility. Rules can change, so ask the state what applies today and ask any company whether it is registered or certified.

If you want to know whether a repair after cleanup needs a permit, call the Village of Wheeling Community Development Department at (847) 459-2620. Nearby, the pages for Prospect Heights and Lincolnshire list their own building offices.

Testing: when it helps and when it does not

Testing can tell you what is in the air or on a surface, but it cannot tell you whether a building is safe or whether you need removal. Many jobs proceed on a visual inspection and moisture readings alone. Testing becomes useful when a buyer, lender or landlord asks for it, or when you need a clear before-and-after record.

If testing is used for clearance, the tester should be independent of the remediation company. That separation keeps the result honest. Ask the party requesting the test what they require before you pay for one.

What a professional visit looks like

  • A walk-through with moisture readings and photographs
  • A written scope that names the rooms and materials involved
  • Containment with plastic barriers and negative air
  • Removal of affected porous materials and cleaning of hard surfaces
  • HEPA vacuuming, a final visual check and drying confirmation

You should receive the scope in writing before work starts, along with a price explained in plain terms.

Common questions

Does bleach kill black mold?

The EPA page we reviewed recommends detergent and water on hard surfaces. It does not tell homeowners to rely on bleach. Dry the area fully afterward.

Does vinegar work?

It is a popular home method, but we have no primary source to cite for it. Scrubbing with detergent and water and drying completely is the EPA approach.

Can mold come back after cleaning?

Yes, if moisture returns. We cannot promise it will not come back, which is why fixing the water source comes first.

Is black mold more dangerous than other kinds?

We do not make health claims. Ask a physician about symptoms, and treat any significant growth the same way: find the moisture and remove affected material.
