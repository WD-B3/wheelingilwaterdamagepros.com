A Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill: Wheeling, IL Homeowner Guide

A bill that suddenly doubles is often the first sign of a leak you cannot see. Here is how to confirm it, who to call and what to do if it has already caused damage.

Rule out the easy explanations

Before you assume a leak, check the usual suspects. Guests, filling a pool, watering a new lawn and a change in billing period can all raise a bill. A bill that covers more days than usual will also be higher. Compare the dates and the usage line, not just the total.

If nothing in your routine changed and the number is still high, the next step is a leak check. Many leaks run silently for weeks, inside a wall, under a slab or in a toilet that runs when no one is looking.

A simple leak test

1

Shut everything off

Turn off all taps and appliances that use water, including the ice maker and washing machine.

2

Read the meter

Note the reading or watch the small leak indicator on the meter.

3

Wait

Wait at least thirty minutes with no water used, or longer if you can.

4

Read again

If the number moved or the indicator keeps spinning, water is flowing somewhere.

A moving meter with everything off points to a leak between the meter and the house or inside the house. Shut off the main valve and see if the meter stops. If it stops, the leak is inside.

Common hidden sources

  • A toilet flapper that lets water seep past
  • A leaking water heater tank or relief valve
  • An irrigation line or outdoor spigot
  • A pinhole in a supply line behind a wall
  • A leak in the line from the meter to the house

A toilet leak is the easiest to test. Put a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait without flushing. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

Who maintains what

In Wheeling, the Village maintains the water mains, and property owners maintain the private service line that connects to them. A leak in the private line is your responsibility, and a plumber is the right call. If you suspect the Village side, call Village water billing. We handle the water damage the leak leaves behind.

Slab leaks are one special case. Water under a concrete floor can raise your bill and also raise floor temperature or cause damp spots. See slab leak water damage for what the crew does.

If the leak already caused damage

Stains on a ceiling, soft drywall, warped flooring and a musty smell all suggest the water has been running a while. Stop the water at the main, call a plumber and call us for extraction and drying. Document everything for your insurer. Read about burst pipes and water heater leaks for the process.

Check your policy. Sudden failures are often covered, while a slow, long-running leak often is not. Ask your insurer how yours is treated.

A Wheeling note

The Village of Wheeling offers AquaHawk Alerting, a free service for Village water customers. It lets you monitor your usage, view projected bills and get notified when consumption suggests abnormal use. Alerts can come by email, text or telephone, and you register at wheeil.aquahawk.us.

The Village water billing phone is 847-459-2607 and the email is waterbilling@wheelingil.gov. Ask about your account and your meter readings. Neighbors in Lincolnshire buy water from Highland Park and should ask their own Village. In Prospect Heights, the City Water Department operates the local system.

Stopping it from happening again

  • Sign up for usage alerts if your Village offers them
  • Check the meter monthly and write the reading down
  • Replace worn toilet flappers and old supply hoses
  • Know where your main shutoff is and test it yearly
  • Look under sinks and behind appliances every few months

Reading the bill itself

Look at the usage number, not only the dollar total. Compare it with the same period last year and the months before. A steady climb can point to a slow leak, while a single jump may point to an event, such as a filled pool or a running toilet. Note the meter read dates, since estimated reads can distort a single bill.

Checking appliances and fixtures

Washers, dishwashers, ice makers and water softeners all connect to supply lines. Look for dampness, mineral crust at joints and a sound of running water when nothing is on. Check under sinks and behind the refrigerator. Our appliance leak page describes the damage a failed line can cause.

Seasonal checks

Outdoor spigots and irrigation lines can leak quietly in the growing season. In cold weather, exposed pipes can freeze and split, and the leak may start when they thaw. Read about frozen pipe water damage and shut off outdoor lines before the first hard freeze.

What to tell the plumber and the crew

When you call a plumber, share the meter test result, the date the bill changed and anywhere you saw dampness. When you call us, add the rooms you think are wet and whether the water is still running. Those details tell the crew which meters and equipment to bring.

If you shut off the main water, say so. If you could not, say that too. We will walk you through what to do until the plumber arrives.

Common questions

Will the Village adjust my bill for a leak?

Ask Village water billing at 847-459-2607. Policies vary and we cannot promise an adjustment.

How do I know it is a slab leak?

Warm spots on the floor, the sound of running water and a high bill are signs, and a plumber can confirm it.

Does homeowners insurance cover a leak that raised my bill?

Sudden failures are often covered, while long, slow leaks often are not. Check your policy.

Should I shut off the main?

Yes, if the meter keeps moving with everything off, then call a plumber.
