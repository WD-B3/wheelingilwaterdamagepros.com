Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage in Wheeling, IL?

Drying a wet house usually does not need a permit, but rebuilding often does. Rules differ by town, so this guide shows what each local office says and what to ask.

Drying versus rebuilding

Extraction, drying and cleaning are different from replacing or altering parts of a building. Permits come into play when you replace drywall, change plumbing or electrical, or install a water heater. Because rules vary by town and change over time, the safe step is always to call the local building office before work starts.

Our wording follows each town's own pages. When a page does not say, we say so and tell you to ask. We do not guess at permit rules.

What each local office says

TownOfficeWhat its page says
WheelingCommunity Development Department, (847) 459-2620Lists water heater installation, plumbing work and electrical work as permit-required, and says to call about other items
Prospect HeightsBuilding Department, City Hall, 847-398-6070 ext. 211Says re-drywalling, plumbing, water heater replacement and electrical work need permits, and says painting, carpeting and tiling do not
Mount ProspectCommunity Development DepartmentSays any alteration to a building or land needs a permit, and floodplain construction needs a Floodplain Development Permit
LincolnshireBuilding Division, Village main line 847-883-8600Has a Building Division page, so call the Village to ask what your repair needs

These are summaries of what the pages say, not legal advice. Call before you start. A short call can save you a stop-work order or a failed inspection.

Jobs that often come up after water damage

  • Replacing drywall, which Prospect Heights says needs a permit
  • Replacing a water heater, which Wheeling and Prospect Heights list as permit-required
  • Plumbing repairs, which both towns list
  • Electrical repairs and panel work, which both list
  • Finish work like painting, carpeting and tile, which Prospect Heights says does not

Your contractor often pulls the permit for their own trade. Ask who is responsible, and make sure it is written in the scope of work.

Floodplains change the answer

The Village of Mount Prospect says construction in a floodplain needs a Floodplain Development Permit under Chapter 22 of the Village Code, and it says alterations to a building or land need a permit from the Community Development Department. If your property is near a creek, ask the Village how the flood map applies before you rebuild. Use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center to look up your address.

Lincolnshire says it follows the Lake County Watershed Development Ordinance for projects, and it is in Lake County, not Cook County. County rules can apply on top of village rules, so a Lincolnshire homeowner should ask the Village about both.

What to bring when you call

  • Your address and parcel details
  • A short description of the damage and the repair
  • The trade contractors you plan to use
  • Photographs of the damage
  • Your scope of work, if you have one

Ask whether an inspection is needed, how to schedule it and how long a permit takes. Write down the name of the person you spoke to and the date.

How a permit fits with drying

Drying should not wait for a permit. The sooner the structure is dry, the fewer materials need replacing. Permits apply to the rebuild, which comes after readings show the structure is dry. That is why our written records include moisture readings. They show the building official and your insurer what condition the structure was in when repairs began. See structural drying for how dryness is confirmed.

If you are replacing wet drywall, the wall and drywall page describes how the work is staged. For water heaters, see water heater leak cleanup.

A Wheeling note

The Village of Wheeling Community Development Department lists its hours as 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday, and the main Village phone is 847-459-2600. Village Hall is at 2 Community Boulevard.

Wheeling's page names water heaters, plumbing and electrical but does not list drywall, flooring or painting. For those, the page tells residents to call the department, so ask about each item before you buy materials.

Inspections and what they look for

Prospect Heights lists inspections for underground plumbing, rough plumbing and final plumbing, and for electric service, rough electric and final electric. Those stages show what an inspector expects: work seen before walls close, then a final check. If you plan to close up drywall after a plumbing repair, schedule the rough inspection before the wall goes back.

Closing a wall too early is the common mistake. An inspector may ask you to open it again. The sequence matters, so ask your building office which inspections apply and in what order.

Planning the order of work

  • Stop the water and start drying
  • Confirm dry readings in writing
  • Call the building office about permits
  • Complete plumbing or electrical work with inspections
  • Close walls, then finish and paint

Following this order keeps drying ahead of rebuilding. It also keeps the paperwork in step with the work.

If a contractor says no permit is needed

Contractors sometimes say a permit is not needed for small jobs. Sometimes that is true. Ask them to explain why, then confirm with the town. A phone call to the building office takes a few minutes and puts the answer in your own hands. Write down the name of the person you spoke with, the date and what they said.

If you rent or live in a condominium, your landlord or association may also have rules for repairs. Check the building rules in addition to the town rules.

Common questions

Do I need a permit to dry my basement?

Drying itself is not usually the permit question. Rebuilding is. Call your town's building office to confirm.

Does painting need a permit?

Prospect Heights says painting does not. Wheeling's page does not say, so call.

Who pulls the permit?

Usually the contractor for that trade, but agree on it in writing.

What if I skip the permit?

That can lead to stop-work orders or problems at sale or with insurance. Ask your town what applies.
