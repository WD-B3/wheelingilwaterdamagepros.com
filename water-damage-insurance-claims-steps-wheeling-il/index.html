Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Wheeling, IL Homes

A water damage claim goes smoother when the first hours are organized. This guide walks through the steps in the order they usually happen, with the Illinois Department of Insurance guidance that applies.

Step 1: make it safe and stop the source

Before any claim paperwork, handle safety. Turn off the main water valve if a pipe is the source. If water is touching outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out and call your electric utility or 911. Keep children and pets away from sewage or floodwater.

Insurers generally expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Shutting off water, removing standing water and starting drying fits that expectation. Waiting for an adjuster before touching anything can let a small loss grow.

Step 2: document before you move anything

Take wide photos of each affected room, then close-ups of the source and the damage. Photograph the ceiling, walls, floors and any belongings. Video works too. Write down when you noticed the water and what you think caused it.

  • Photos of the source, such as the failed pipe or appliance
  • Photos of every wet room and surface
  • A list of damaged belongings with approximate ages
  • The date and time you first saw water
  • Receipts for any emergency repairs or supplies

Keep removed materials if you can safely store them, since an adjuster may want to see them. If something is contaminated or unsafe to keep, photograph it first.

Step 3: call your insurer

The Illinois Department of Insurance advises contacting your agent or insurer as soon as possible after a loss. The Department does not give a fixed filing deadline on that page, so the safest approach is to call quickly and ask your insurer what applies. For contents, it notes that most policies require replacing them within a specified time from the date of loss.

Ask for your claim number, the adjuster's contact details and what documents they want. Write down the date and name of everyone you speak with.

Step 4: get the water out and dry

Water extraction and drying should start quickly. The water extraction page describes how standing water is removed, and structural drying covers what happens inside walls and floors. We provide written records of work performed, including moisture readings.

We do not contact your insurer on your behalf as a claim decision-maker, and we never promise approval. We give you documents. You and your insurer take it from there.

Step 5: the adjuster visit and estimates

The adjuster looks at the damage and prepares an estimate. Your restoration scope may not match it line for line. That is common and usually resolved by comparing documents. If you disagree, the Department suggests checking your policy for an appraisal clause, and other options include a lawyer or a public adjuster.

If you hire a public adjuster, the Department says the contract can only be canceled by certified mail within five business days after it was signed. Read any contract before signing.

Step 6: repairs and closeout

After drying is confirmed by readings, repairs begin. Some work needs a permit. Keep the written scope, the drying logs and receipts together in one folder. They help if questions come up later.

A Wheeling note

In Wheeling, permits are required for water heater installation, plumbing work and electrical work according to the Village permit page, and the Community Development Department at (847) 459-2620 answers questions about other items. The department is open 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., Monday through Friday.

If your loss involves a replaced water heater or rewired outlets, ask about permits early so the repair does not stall your claim paperwork.

If you disagree with your insurer

The Department lets consumers file a complaint through its message center or by calling (866) 445-5364. Keep your documents handy when you call. Nearby residents of Lincolnshire and Prospect Heights follow the same state process.

What to say on the first call

Keep it factual. Give the date, the likely cause, what rooms are affected and what emergency steps you have taken. Do not guess at causes you are not sure of, and do not agree to a settlement figure on the first call. Ask what documents the insurer wants and how they prefer to receive photos.

Mistakes that slow a claim

  • Throwing away damaged items before photographing them
  • Waiting days to call the insurer
  • Starting permanent repairs before the adjuster has looked
  • Losing receipts for emergency work
  • Not keeping a log of who you talked to and when

None of these end a claim by themselves, but each one makes it harder. A simple folder, paper or digital, solves most of them.

Keeping a claim file

Use one folder for everything: the policy, photos, the claim number, estimates, receipts and a short log of calls. Add the drying logs and the written scope of work when you receive them. If a question comes up months later, a complete file answers it in minutes.

Common questions

How soon should I report water damage?

As soon as possible. The Illinois Department of Insurance advises contacting your agent or insurer promptly after a loss.

Should I wait for the adjuster before cleaning up?

No. Stop the source and begin drying while documenting everything. Keep damaged items until you know whether the adjuster wants to see them.

Does the restoration company file my claim?

No. You file the claim. We provide documents such as photos, readings and a written scope of work.

Can the Wheeling Community Development Department help with my claim?

It handles permits, not insurance. Call it for permit questions at (847) 459-2620.
