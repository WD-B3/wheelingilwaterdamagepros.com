How to Get Rid of Mold in a House in Wheeling, IL

Getting rid of mold is two jobs. One is removing the growth. The other, and the one that decides whether it returns, is removing the moisture. This guide covers both.

Fix the water first

Mold needs moisture, so cleaning without fixing the source is a short-term fix. Find the leak, the condensation or the humidity that fed it. Repair the pipe, seal the gap or run a dehumidifier in a damp basement. Then deal with the growth.

The EPA puts it plainly: fix plumbing leaks and other water problems as soon as possible, and dry all items completely. Anything wet for more than a day or two is a candidate for growth, so drying quickly is the best prevention.

Small areas you can handle

The EPA describes an area less than about 10 square feet, roughly a three-foot by three-foot patch, as one a homeowner can often clean. For hard surfaces, it recommends scrubbing with detergent and water and drying completely. Wear gloves, eye protection and a mask rated for the job, and keep the area ventilated.

  • Wipe hard, non-porous surfaces with detergent and water
  • Dry the surface completely, with a fan or dehumidifier
  • Bag and discard cleaning rags
  • Do not paint or caulk over mold, since paint over mold is likely to peel
  • Watch the spot for a few weeks to see if it returns

If it comes back, the moisture problem is not fixed, and cleaning again will not help. The cause needs to be found.

What usually has to be removed

Porous materials are different. The EPA notes that items like ceiling tiles and carpet may need to be discarded because mold can grow into their empty spaces and may be impossible to remove fully. Drywall with growth, wet insulation and damaged carpet pad usually come out. Wood framing can often be cleaned and dried.

When to call a professional

Call when the area is large, when the water was sewage or other contaminated water, when the heating and cooling system may be affected or when you cannot find the source. The EPA gives the same triggers. A professional contains the area with plastic barriers, uses negative air machines with HEPA filters and removes affected material without spreading spores to clean rooms.

Remediation follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. Ask about experience and references, and ask for a written scope. Our process is described on the mold remediation page.

Keeping it from coming back

  • Fix leaks quickly and dry wet materials fully
  • Run bathroom fans and vent dryers outside
  • Keep basement humidity under control with a dehumidifier
  • Check under sinks and around appliances every few months
  • Make sure gutters and downspouts carry water away from the foundation

No one can promise that mold will never return. If moisture returns, growth can too. Control the moisture and you control the problem.

A Wheeling note

Illinois passed Public Act 103-0893, effective January 1, 2025, which amends the Mold Remediation Registration Act. As we read it, the Department of Public Health may create a registration program that asks for third-party certification and proof of financial responsibility. Ask the state what is required today, and ask any company whether it is registered or certified.

For permit questions on repairs after remediation, the Village of Wheeling Community Development Department is at (847) 459-2620.

Protective gear and technique

Wear gloves, eye protection and a respirator or mask suited to the task. Mist the surface lightly so you do not stir up dust, and clean from the edge of the patch toward the center. Bag debris in plastic before carrying it through the house, and wash your clothes afterward.

What not to do

  • Do not dry-brush or sand moldy drywall
  • Do not run a fan that blows across the growth into clean rooms
  • Do not mix cleaning chemicals
  • Do not cover growth with paint or caulk
  • Do not ignore the leak because the spot looks small

If you are not sure the area is as small as it looks, open a small hole and check. Mold behind a wall can be much larger than the stain on the surface.

Common questions

Can bleach get rid of mold?

The EPA page we reviewed recommends detergent and water on hard surfaces and does not recommend relying on bleach. Dry the area completely afterward.

Do I need a pro for a small spot?

Often not, if it is under about 10 square feet and the source is fixed. Call for larger areas or contaminated water.

How long does remediation take?

It depends on the area and the materials. We give a written scope and timeline after an inspection.

Will my insurance cover mold removal?

Many policies limit mold coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer.
