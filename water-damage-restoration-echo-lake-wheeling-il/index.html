A pond-and-townhome part of Wheeling

The Village Stormwater Master Plan treats Echo Lake and Ridgefield as one drainage area of about 982 acres, with detention ponds at Echo Lake, North and South Ridgefield, Polo Run and Sandpebble. Ridgefield sits on Wheeling Road south of Hintz Road, and a real estate directory lists single-family homes and townhouses there, built from about 1978 to 1993.

The plan says the ponds sit in historically flood-prone areas and were required to keep floodplain storage. Its main concern was shoreline erosion and silt that reduced pond depth. In its 100-year, 24-hour model it showed the Sandpebble parking lot and Bridle Trail partly under water, which is a model result and not a report of damage.

When the water comes from a neighbor

In a townhome or condo, water damage often starts in someone else's unit. A leaking heater above can wet your ceiling, light fixtures and walls. We map where the water traveled, because a repair that ignores the source unit often fails. Water heater leaks and appliance leaks are the usual causes.

What the Village records show about the ponds

The plan used the September 13 and 14, 2008 storm to calibrate its Echo Lake and Ridgefield model, and the Cook County hazard annex lists an Echo Lake storm sewer near Hintz Road among Wheeling drainage features. Neither source says how many homes here have been flooded, and we will not guess a number.

The practical point is maintenance. If you own or rent in an association with ponds, ask who maintains them and how often. For a drainage concern, contact the association and the Village. For water already inside, call us.

Associations, permits and insurance

Many attached communities have a master policy and rules about who repairs what. Ask your association and your insurer, because we cannot say who is responsible. The Village Community Development office at (847) 459-2620 handles permits for plumbing and electrical repairs. Whether any building here sits in a mapped flood area is not something we have verified.

Echo Lake area questions

Is Echo Lake a neighborhood or a drainage area?

The Village plan uses Echo Lake and Ridgefield as a drainage area name. Residents use Ridgefield, Polo Run and Echo Lake for nearby communities, and we serve all of them.

Does the plan say these homes flood?

It says the ponds are in historically flood-prone areas and models parking lots flooding. It does not give a count of flooded homes.

Who pays when a neighbor leaks into my unit?

That depends on your association rules and insurance. We document the damage and do not decide responsibility.

Do you work in condos and townhomes?

Yes. We ask for the association contact and any building access rules at the start of the call.

Can you dry a ceiling without removing it?

Sometimes. We use moisture readings to decide whether drying in place is possible.
