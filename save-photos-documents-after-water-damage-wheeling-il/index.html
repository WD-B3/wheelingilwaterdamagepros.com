Saving Photos and Documents After Water Damage in Wheeling, IL

Wet paper can often be saved if you move fast and avoid making it worse. Here is a calm, step-by-step approach for photographs, important papers and books.

First, stay safe and keep calm

Before you reach for anything, make sure the area is safe. Do not walk through water near outlets, and stay out of contaminated water entirely. If a sewage backup soaked the papers, treat them as contaminated, wear gloves and ask a professional before handling them.

Once the area is safe, work in order of importance. Birth certificates, deeds, passports, tax files and irreplaceable photographs come first. Everything else can wait a few hours.

Photographs

Wet photographs stick together as they dry, so do not let them dry in a stack. Handle them by the edges. If they are stuck together, do not pull. Soak gently in clean, cool water until they separate, then lay them face up on a clean absorbent surface.

  • Handle by the edges, with clean hands
  • Rinse mud gently in clean cool water
  • Lay face up on paper towels or a clean towel
  • Change blotting material as it soaks
  • Keep them out of direct sun and away from heat

If you cannot deal with them in a day or two, freeze them in a freezer bag. Freezing pauses the damage so you can sort later.

Documents and books

For paper documents, separate wet pages if you can do so without tearing. Interleave with clean paper and press gently. For a book, lay it flat and stand it on its end with the pages fanned out only if it is lightly damp. A heavily soaked book is better frozen and dealt with later.

Making copies

Once dry, scan or photograph the items right away. A copy protects against any later damage. For certified records such as birth certificates and deeds, ask the issuing office how to get replacements. Take notes now about what was lost so you can recreate lists later.

When to call a specialist

Valuable photographs, rare books and documents of historical value may justify a conservator or a document recovery company. We are a water damage restoration crew and our main job is to dry the building. We can pack out contents and tell you what is worth sending to a specialist.

The drying of the building matters here too. Paper kept in a humid room can grow mold. See the structural drying page for how crews bring a room back to a normal reading.

A Wheeling note

The Village of Mount Prospect notes on its flood page that its public library has more floodproofing information, and most Illinois libraries can help residents find preservation guidance. A local librarian is a good first stop for general advice on caring for wet books.

For help with permits needed after a loss, Wheeling's Community Development Department is at (847) 459-2620.

Prevention for the next time

  • Keep papers off the floor and out of cardboard boxes
  • Store key documents in a waterproof container on an upper floor
  • Scan important records and keep a copy elsewhere
  • Keep photos in plastic sleeves rather than loose in a basement

Homes near creeks in Mount Prospect and Prospect Heights should put this at the top of their list, because basement storage is usually the first place water reaches. The basement cleanup page explains what the crew does when water gets to stored goods.

A simple drying setup at home

Clear a table and cover it with clean paper or towels. Run a fan in the room so air moves gently across the items, not directly at them. A dehumidifier helps if the air is damp. Set photos face up, spread papers out in single layers and stand books on their ends once they are not dripping.

Check every few hours and replace soaked blotting paper. Work in small batches, so nothing sits wet in a pile. If you run out of space or time, freeze the rest.

Labeling and organizing

As items dry, label them with a pencil on the back of the photo border or on a sticky note. Group by event or year so you can find things later. Keep a list of which documents were damaged and which agencies need to reissue them. A short list now saves hours later.

Documents that need special handling

Inked and printed pages react differently to water. Some inks run, glossy paper sticks and old photographs can shed their emulsion. Do not rub or scrape, and avoid direct sun. If a page is fragile, lift it on a sheet of clean paper rather than your fingers. Copy anything legally important before you touch it more.

Common questions

Can I use a hair dryer on wet photos?

No. Heat can warp photos and make them stick. Air drying is gentler.

How long can wet paper wait before mold appears?

It varies, but freezing within a day or two stops the damage while you plan.

Can a restoration company recover documents?

We dry the building and can pack out contents. For valuable documents, a specialist document recovery company may be the better route.

Can I save a wet hard drive?

Do not power it on. Ask a data recovery specialist.
