Water Damaged Electronics: When to Unplug in Wheeling, IL

Water and electricity do not mix, and the safest rule is to stay out of the water and de-energize only when you can do it dry. This guide explains the order of steps and what to do with wet devices.

Safety comes before the gadgets

If water is touching outlets, power strips, appliances or the panel, do not walk into it. Standing in water while touching a plug is exactly how people get shocked. Call your electric utility or 911 for any shock risk and wait for help. A television is not worth it.

Shut off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry, you can reach it without crossing water and you are sure of the right breaker. If any of that is uncertain, stay out and call. The same goes for a basement where water is rising toward outlets.

When it is safe to unplug

  • The floor around the outlet is dry and you are not standing in water
  • Your hands are dry
  • The plug and cord are not wet
  • You can reach the plug without moving through water

If a device itself is wet but the outlet and floor are dry, unplug it by gripping the plug, not the cord. Do not press power buttons or try to see if it still works. Powering on a wet device can short it.

What to do with wet devices

Drying a device in open air in a warm, dry room helps, but internal moisture can sit inside for days. A technician can open a device and check it. For important data, ask a data recovery specialist before powering anything on.

Cords, strips and outlets

Extension cords and power strips that were under water should be thrown out. Wet outlets and switches should be checked by a licensed electrician before they are used. Water can corrode contacts, and a damp outlet can become a hazard weeks later.

If water reached the electrical panel, do not touch it. Call your electric utility and an electrician. For appliances like a washer or dishwasher that failed and leaked, read about appliance leak water damage.

Moving electronics out of the way

If water is spreading and you can do so safely, lift electronics to a higher shelf or another room. That includes routers and game consoles on the floor. Move them only after you have unplugged them from a dry outlet. If that is not possible, leave them and let the crew handle it after power is off.

Our crews work around electronics carefully and keep power off in wet rooms. For cases that involve ceilings or walls above equipment, see ceiling water damage repair.

A Wheeling note

The Village of Wheeling lists electrical work among the jobs that need a permit, and the Community Development Department at (847) 459-2620 can answer questions about other items. If a wet outlet or panel is replaced after a loss, ask about a permit before work starts.

Basements near creeks and rivers in the area can fill with water at floor level, and outlets near the floor are the first at risk. Call your electric utility, not just a restoration company, for any panel or meter that has been wet.

After the drying

Once the room has been dried and the electrician has cleared the circuit, devices can be tested one at a time. Keep the cords in view and watch for odd smells or warmth. Neighbors in Lincolnshire and Prospect Heights use the same safe order.

Home office and business equipment

Computers, printers and network gear often sit on floors or low shelves, which makes them early casualties. If water reaches them, power them down only from a dry outlet and then leave them off. Back up data after the device has been checked, not before, and never run a wet drive to copy files.

Businesses with servers or point-of-sale systems should call their IT provider early and tell the restoration crew what equipment is in the area. Our commercial water damage page explains how crews plan around equipment and open hours.

After a power interruption

Storms that bring water in can also interrupt power. When power returns, a surge can hit devices that were left plugged in. Unplug sensitive electronics before a storm if you have time, and plug them back in one at a time once the power is steady.

Signs a device should not be used

  • A burning or electrical smell
  • Fogging behind a screen
  • Corrosion or white residue on ports
  • A device that gets hot while idle
  • A cord or plug that feels damp

Any of these means the device should stay unplugged until a technician checks it. A repair shop can often tell you quickly whether it is worth fixing.

Common questions

Should I turn off the breaker for a wet basement?

Only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water. Otherwise call your electric utility or 911.

Is rice good for a wet phone?

We have no source that says it works. Power it off, dry the outside and ask a repair shop.

Can I reuse a power strip that got wet?

No. Discard it after water contact.

Who checks wet outlets?

A licensed electrician. Ask the Village about permits for any replacement work.
