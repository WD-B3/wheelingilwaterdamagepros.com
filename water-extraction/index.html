When standing water is an emergency

Emergency water extraction in Wheeling, IL is the work of pulling standing water off floors with pumps and extraction units. If you have more than a puddle, treat it as urgent and call the 24/7 line. While you wait, shut off the main water valve if the source is a supply line, and keep everyone out of rooms where water has reached outlets or the electrical panel.

Water that sits does more harm each hour. It wicks up drywall, swells baseboards and pushes into the subfloor. A few inches in a finished room can mean a full structural drying job, but a fast pump-out often limits how much has to be torn out. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and we never promise an arrival window.

Stop the source

If a pipe or appliance is still running, close its shut-off or the main valve.

Power first

Do not wade through water near outlets or an energized panel. Call your electric utility or 911 for any shock risk.

Lift valuables

Move electronics, papers and cardboard above the waterline. Leave heavy furniture alone.

Note the depth

Take a photo with a ruler or tape beside the water. It helps the estimate and your claim.

How water extraction works

Extraction starts with the water that is easy to see and moves to the water that is not. A truck-mounted or portable extraction unit pulls water from hard floors, while weighted wands pull it out of carpet and pad. Submersible pumps handle depths of several inches, and the crew checks that the pump discharge is directed away from the house.

Afterward, moisture meters and thermal imaging find the water that moved into walls and under floors. This is where extraction hands over to structural drying and dehumidification, because pumping alone leaves the building material wet. If the water came from a drain or toilet overflow, the job changes to sewage backup cleanup and the safety steps change with it.

What drives the price of extraction

Water removal is priced by the job. These are the factors that move the number:

  • Depth of water and the square footage it covers
  • Water category, meaning clean, gray or contaminated
  • Whether the water reached carpet, pad or a finished basement
  • Access for hoses and pumps, such as stairs or a sealed crawl space
  • How much drying equipment the structure needs afterward

Questions about water removal

How quickly does the water have to come out?

As soon as possible. Water that sits for a day or more soaks into building materials that could have been saved with an early pump-out. If you cannot tell how deep the water is, call anyway and describe what you see.

Can you remove water from a finished basement?

Yes. Finished basements often have carpet, pad and drywall that need a closer look after the water is out. See the basement page for what gets checked and what often gets replaced.

Do I need to rent equipment myself?

Not for a covered loss through us. Equipment is part of the job scope, and we name each piece in plain words in the written estimate so you know what is running in your home.

Do you handle water extraction emergencies at night or on weekends in Wheeling?

Yes. The line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays. Tell us how deep the water is and where it came from, switch off the source if it is safe to reach, and we will send a crew as soon as one is free. We do not promise an arrival time.
