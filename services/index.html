Start with where the water came from

Water damage restoration in Wheeling, IL is easiest to understand from the source. A burst pipe, a failed water heater and a storm need different first steps, even though the end of every job looks alike: the water is out, the structure is dry and the damage is repaired. The sections below sort the services by cause.

If you are not sure what caused the loss, the answer is usually in the first photographs. Look for where the water is darkest, where it is dripping and where a stain is growing. Then call and describe what you see. A meter reading beats a guess.

Pipes and plumbing

Supply lines fail in every season. The most common calls are a burst pipe, a frozen pipe that split in a cold snap, a leaking water heater and a slab leak under a concrete floor. Each page explains the shutoff, the likely hidden spread and what the cleanup involves.

Appliances, cooling and kitchens

Small leaks are easy to miss until a floor lifts or a ceiling stains. Read about appliance leaks from washers, dishwashers and refrigerators, an AC condensate leak and the kitchen cabinet and floor damage that follows a slow drip under the sink.

Weather, flooding and the building envelope

Heavy rain and melting snow bring a different set of problems. Start with storm water damage and flood damage cleanup. A wet basement and a damp crawl space each have their own page, and a stained ceiling often points to a roof or an upstairs leak.

Removing and drying the water

Every job needs the water out and the structure dry. Water extraction removes standing water first, and structural drying and dehumidification dry what is left in walls, floors and cavities. A sewage backup is treated as contaminated water and handled with protective gear.

Floors and walls

Flooring and walls decide how much of the loss can be saved. See hardwood floors, carpet and pad and walls and drywall for what can be dried in place and what usually has to be replaced.

Commercial properties and mold

Shops, offices and multi-unit buildings have deadlines a house does not, so commercial water damage has its own page. If water sat for days and you see dark spots or smell something musty, read about mold remediation, which follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. We do not quote a number over the phone without seeing the loss, because two wet rooms can be very different jobs. Industry practice follows ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.
