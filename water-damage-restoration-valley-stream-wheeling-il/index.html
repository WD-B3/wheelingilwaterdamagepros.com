Valley Stream and Buffalo Creek

Valley Stream sits in northwest Wheeling, west of Elmhurst Road and north of Dundee Road. A real estate directory lists condos, townhouses and houses built from about 1962 to 1969 along Valley Stream Drive. The Village Stormwater Master Plan also names nearby Saint Armand Lane, Beverly Drive, Woodland Drive, Cedar Drive, Pebble Drive and Wilshire Drive.

The plan says Buffalo Creek overtops its banks and floods the residential area to the south. It places part of this study area in the FEMA 100-year regulatory floodplain, and it notes that homes in that floodplain with a mortgage must carry flood insurance. That does not mean every house here is in the floodplain, so check your own address.

What changed upstream, and what did not

The Metropolitan Water Reclamation District expanded the Buffalo Creek Reservoir in Buffalo Grove, with a ceremony on July 28, 2022. It said the work added storage of 58.6 million gallons, removed 107 structures from flooding impacts and benefited more than 2,000 structures along Buffalo Creek and the Des Plaines River. Wheeling was named a downstream beneficiary.

That is a regional benefit. It does not promise that a given Valley Stream street stays dry. On August 19, 2025, after about 1.5 inches of rain, the Village reported high levels in Buffalo Creek and Lake Heritage and ran pumps. Watching the creek on heavy rain days is still sensible.

If water enters from the creek side

Creek water is not clean water. It can carry silt, sewage and chemicals, and it can stay on floors long enough to soak drywall and insulation. Stay out of it, keep children and pets away, and call us before you start removing anything. Our flood damage cleanup page explains the cleaning and drying steps.

Do not wade in

Standing water may touch outlets or cords. Wait until power is confirmed off.

Lift what you can

Raise valuables off wet floors only when it is safe to enter the room.

Document it

Photograph the water line on walls and the damaged items.

Keep records

Save receipts and written notes of when you noticed the water.

Condos, townhouses and houses in the same area

Because Valley Stream mixes attached and detached homes, water often travels between units. A burst supply line in one townhome can reach a neighbor through shared walls. We ask early who else is affected, and we coordinate with the owner or association as needed. Read more on burst pipe water damage.

Flood insurance and records

A standard homeowners policy often excludes flood damage, and flood insurance is a separate policy. Ask your insurer what applies to you. We provide photos, readings and a written scope, but we cannot say a claim will be paid. For an address check, call Village Engineering at (847) 459-2620.

Valley Stream questions

Is my Valley Stream home in the FEMA floodplain?

The plan says part of the study area is. Whether your home is depends on your address, so ask Village Engineering or use the FEMA Flood Map Service Center.

Does the Buffalo Creek reservoir stop flooding here?

No source says it does. The district reported regional flood reduction, and we cannot say what that means for one street.

Can you work if a street is flooded?

Tell us which route is open. We plan around closures and do not promise arrival times.

What if my condo association handles the building?

Call the association as well. We will document what we find in your unit.

Should I clean up creek water myself?

Not before a professional looks. Creek water can carry contaminants, so ordinary mops and fans are not enough.
