Lincolnshire is split by the Des Plaines River

The Des Plaines River runs north to south through Lincolnshire and divides the village near Village Hall. Water damage here is a river-and-creek story as much as a plumbing one. The Village lists three main flood hazard areas, the Des Plaines River, Indian Creek and the West Fork of the North Branch of the Chicago River.

The Village also says it cannot prevent flooding, but it works with developers, follows the Lake County Watershed Development Ordinance and takes part in the National Flood Insurance Program. Homeowners can follow river levels on the National Weather Service website and use an inundation map built with the Lake County Stormwater Management Commission and USGS.

Reading the river before it reaches the house

Check the gauge on the days the river is rising. If your street is near the water, a rising reading is a reason to lift valuables from low floors and to call us early, not after the carpet is soaked.

The FEMA map for your own address is on the Village website, and the Building Division can answer questions about what applies to your lot.

If water is already inside

Lincolnshire sits in Lake County, not Cook County, which changes the local offices but not the first hour. Shut off the source, keep power off wet rooms if the panel is dry, and call our 24/7 line from anywhere in the 60069 ZIP code. We come up Milwaukee Avenue from Wheeling.

Find the source

Turn off the main valve or the fixture valve. If the river or a creek is the source, stay out of the water.

Mind the electric

Leave basement outlets alone if the floor is wet. Call your electric utility if cords or the panel are in water.

Lift and move

Raise papers, shoes and cords off the floor. Photograph before you move anything.

Protect people

Children and pets stay out of dirty water. Sewage needs gloves, boots and a mask.

Where we reach in Lincolnshire

Milwaukee Avenue, which is U.S. 45 and Illinois 21, runs through the village, and Half Day Road is Illinois 22. Aptakisic Road is another main route, and Interstate 94, the Tri-State Tollway, forms part of the east border. Those are the roads we use to reach homes near Stevenson High School, the Marriott Theatre and the Village Hall.

If the road in front of a house is closed by high water, tell us which route you can use. We will plan around it. A crew that arrives with the right pumps and meters beats a crew that arrives sooner with the wrong equipment.

Water supply and sewer in Lincolnshire

The Village says it buys Lake Michigan water from the City of Highland Park, a connection that began in 1982. Sanitary sewage is treated by the Lake County Department of Public Works and discharged to the Des Plaines River. For a suspected problem with Village-owned utilities, Public Works takes calls at 847-913-2380.

The Village advises hiring a licensed plumber for a water or sewer problem on private property, and calling Public Works if Village utilities may be involved. That is the split we would follow too. We dry and clean, the plumber fixes the pipe, and the Village handles its own mains.

Permits and the Building Division

Lincolnshire's Building Division is part of the Village. The Village main line is 847-883-8600, open Monday to Friday from 8:30 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and Village Hall is at 1 Olde Half Day Road. Permit rules differ by project, so ask the Division before drywall, plumbing or electrical work begins.

Because Lincolnshire is in Lake County, county-level rules for stormwater and watershed development apply to some projects. A restoration crew does not make that call. Your contractor and the Village do, and our written scope helps both of them.
