Spring rain and the April 2013 flood

The Village of Mount Prospect says five waterways carry stormwater through town, the Des Plaines River, McDonald Creek, Feehanville Ditch, Weller Creek and Higgins Creek. Each has a flood hazard area. The Village flood page lists April 2013 as its most recent flood, after record rainfall fell in a very short time.

A spring downpour is the season to expect basement water here. Frozen pipes belong to a different season, but they follow the same rules. Shut off the source, get the water out and dry the structure before surfaces start to turn.

What the Village says about flood protection

Village Engineering staff can locate a property on the FEMA Flood Insurance Rate Map and give basic information from it. Alterations to a building or land need a permit from the Community Development Department, and any construction in a floodplain needs a Floodplain Development Permit under Chapter 22 of the Village Code.

The Village page also notes that most homeowner policies do not cover flood losses, that flood insurance is sold through the National Flood Insurance Program, and that a new policy has a 30-day waiting period. That waiting period matters before a wet spring, not during one.

Water supply and who to call

The Village page says source water is drawn from Lake Michigan, treated by the City of Chicago and moved by the Northwest Suburban Municipal Joint Action Water Agency. The Village owns and operates the distribution system for about 42,000 residents, and Illinois American Water Company serves the rest of the community.

Public Works is at 1700 West Central Road, and the number is (847) 870-5640. Call it for suspected Village main problems or to report debris in a creek. For a plumbing fault inside your own walls, a licensed plumber is the right call, and we handle the water that fault leaves behind.

Shopping centers, offices and apartments

Randhurst Village is a shopping center in Mount Prospect, and the town has many other stores, offices and multi-unit buildings. Water that starts in one unit often reaches the next, and a failed sprinkler line or roof drain can hit several tenants at once. Our commercial water damage page explains how we phase work around open businesses.

For homes, the common calls are storm water damage, sewage backup cleanup and basement water damage. If a wall stays wet, the structural drying page shows the equipment and readings we use.
