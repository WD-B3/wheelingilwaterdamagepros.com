Keep or Throw Away After Water Damage: Wheeling, IL Guide

After a flood or leak, the hardest question is what can be saved. The answer depends on the kind of water, how long things stayed wet and what they are made of. This guide gives you a sorting method.

Start with the water, not the stuff

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories. Category 1 is clean water, such as a broken supply line. Category 2 is gray water, such as washing machine or dishwasher overflow. Category 3 is contaminated water, such as sewage or floodwater. The category changes everything you can keep.

Clean water for a short time leaves many things savable. Contaminated water makes porous items nearly impossible to clean safely. When in doubt about the source, treat the water as contaminated until a professional says otherwise.

A sorting guide by material

ItemClean water, caught fastGray or contaminated water
Solid wood furnitureOften dried and keptOften cleaned by a professional, case by case
Upholstered furniture and mattressesSometimes dried, check pad and frameUsually discarded
Carpet and padCarpet often dried, pad often replacedUsually removed
Books and papersFreeze or dry quicklyOften discarded, copies kept
Clothes and linensWash on a hot cycle if possibleWash separately or discard
Drywall and insulationDried in place if recentCut out
Hard goods like glass and metalClean and dryClean and sanitize
Food and medicineDiscard if touchedDiscard

Use the table as a guide, not a rule. A piece of furniture that costs a few hundred dollars may not be worth professional cleaning, while a family heirloom might be.

Photograph before you discard

Before you throw anything away, photograph it and note its approximate age and value. The Illinois Department of Insurance says most policies require you to replace contents within a specified period from the date of loss, and the page does not state that period. Ask your insurer what it is. Keep a list while the memory is fresh.

If an adjuster wants to see an item and it is safe to keep, store it in a garage or a bag. If it is unsafe, photograph it from several angles and ask the adjuster whether that is enough.

What to move right away

  • Wet rugs and loose carpet pieces
  • Cardboard boxes, which wick water and weaken
  • Papers, photos and books, which need quick attention
  • Electronics, which should stay unplugged until checked
  • Clothes in damp piles, which can mildew

Move items to a dry place and spread them out so air can reach them. Do not stack wet items. Our crews also move contents out of the way for extraction and drying, and sometimes pack them out for safe storage.

When things are not worth saving

Porous items soaked by contaminated water, items with growth, and anything with a lingering sewage smell are usually better replaced than cleaned. Spending money to save a low-value item rarely makes sense. If you are unsure, ask the crew. We tell you plainly what we think can be saved.

For carpet specifically, see carpet water damage restoration, and for hardwood see hardwood floors. For a loss that involved sewage, read about sewage backup cleanup before you touch anything.

A Wheeling note

Many Wheeling-area homes have finished basements with carpet, drywall and stored boxes close to the floor. When basement water rises, cardboard and stored paper are usually the first losses, and they are cheap to replace compared with the documents inside. Move papers and photos up first.

If you need help sorting after a loss, the Illinois Department of Insurance consumer hotline is 1-800-444-3338, and the Village Community Development office is at (847) 459-2620 for permit questions about repairs.

A last pass

After the first sort, give yourself a day before making final decisions on sentimental items. Things that seem ruined sometimes turn out to be salvageable by a conservator, and things that look fine may carry odor. Towns like Mount Prospect and Prospect Heights see the same decisions after creek flooding.

Room by room

In a kitchen, discard food and anything porous that touched water, and check under cabinets for hidden wetness. In a bedroom, soaked mattresses and pillows are usually replaced while clothing can often be washed. In a basement, expect to lose boxed storage, carpet and lower drywall, and expect to save plastic bins that stayed sealed.

Living rooms usually come down to upholstery. Frames and hard surfaces can often be cleaned, while cushions and padding hold water and odor. Photograph each room before you start moving things so that the sort has a record behind it.

Cleaning what you keep

Hard goods such as glass, metal and sealed wood can be washed with detergent and water and then dried completely. Fabrics that can be laundered should go through a hot wash if the label allows. Items from sewage-contaminated water need extra care, and many are not worth the effort. If you have any doubt, ask the crew before you wash or bag anything.

Common questions

Can I keep a mattress that got wet?

A mattress soaked by clean water for a short time may be dried, but most are replaced, and one touched by contaminated water is discarded.

Should I throw out food from a flooded kitchen?

Yes, discard food that touched water. Cans can be washed and sanitized in some cases, but ask a food safety source.

Can I save wet photos?

Often, if you act quickly. Separate them gently, rinse clean water off and air dry or freeze them.

How long do I have to decide?

Wet items begin to deteriorate within days, so sort quickly and keep notes for your insurer.
